Thévenin Equivalents

Three short lessons on the most useful simplification in circuit analysis: replacing a whole network with one voltage source and one resistor.

Overview 1 · The big idea 2 · Finding Vth and Rth 3 · Loads and power Quiz Sources

When you connect anything to a circuit — a sensor, a motor, a speaker, the next stage of an amplifier — that device only ever sees two terminals. However complicated the network behind those terminals is, a linear one leaves exactly one signature there. Thévenin's theorem says that signature can always be reproduced by two components: a voltage source and a resistor.[1]
